## District snapshot (ACS 5-year, 2022 vintage)

| Measure | Value | Compare to |
|---|---|---|
| Population | ~779,000 | Median CD is ~770,000 by design (each district sized equal at the decennial) |
| Median household income | **$125,652** | US national median is ~$74,500 |
| Residents in poverty | ~59,000 (~7.6%) | US national poverty rate is ~11.5% |
| Adults with bachelor's degree | ~172,000 | Doesn't include the (large) population with advanced degrees on top of that |

VA-08 is the **highest-income congressional district in Virginia and one of the highest in the country.** The combination of federal salaries, contractor compensation, and a lawyer/lobbyist/consultant base produces an exceptional household income distribution.

## Federal-spending pattern

Three visible regimes in the spending chart:

1. **Pre-2011 growth.** Steady increase through the late-Bush + early-Obama buildup — DoD spending, the Stimulus, post-9/11 contractor expansion.
2. **2011–2015 flat-and-decline.** The Budget Control Act of 2011 imposed sequestration on FY2013+. VA-08 felt this sharply because so much of the local economy is on prime contracts. Federal spending in the district basically didn't grow for half a decade.
3. **Post-2020 step up.** COVID emergency appropriations, then the IRA/CHIPS/IIJA tranche of Biden-era industrial policy. Spending levels are now well above the 2009 stimulus peak.

VA-11 (Loudoun + outer Fairfax — Gerry Connolly's seat) has grown faster than VA-08 over the same period as data-center construction and contractor satellite offices spread west along the Dulles corridor. VA-08's spending base is more mature; VA-11's is the marginal-growth district for the DC contracting economy.

## Labor market resilience

Virginia state unemployment runs about a percentage point below the US headline rate in almost every period. The spread widens during downturns:

- **2008–2010.** US unemployment peaked at 10.0%; Virginia's peak was 7.6%. A 2.4-point gap.
- **2020 COVID.** US peaked at 14.7%; Virginia at 11.3%. A 3.4-point gap.

This isn't because VA is recession-proof — it's because federal employment is roughly 12% of the state workforce (vs ~2% nationally), and the federal payroll doesn't cyclically shed workers the way manufacturing or hospitality do. Federal-contractor employment is more cyclical but still less so than private-sector averages, because most prime contracts have multi-year obligations.

## Why this dashboard

Most "congressional district" data is hard to compose — published as PDF maps, locked behind interactive web tools, or aggregated only to the state level. VA-08 in particular is a useful case study because:

1. The federal-money story is unusually concentrated and visible
2. Demographic + economic outliers (income, education) make it a stress-test of national-vs-local divergence
3. Combined with neighboring districts (VA-11, MD-05, MD-08, DC) it tells a clean DC-metro federal-economy story you can't get from state-level data alone
