## What this shows

Electricity generation is one of the clearest windows on the energy
transition, and it varies enormously by state. These charts plot net
generation by fuel, in terawatt-hours (TWh), for the country as a
whole and for two of the largest generating states.

The **national** chart tells the headline story: coal in long decline,
overtaken by natural gas around 2016, with wind and solar climbing from
negligible to major contributors.

**Texas** generates more electricity than any other state. Gas leads,
but Texas also runs the largest wind fleet in the country by a wide
margin, with utility-scale solar now scaling fast.

**California** leans on natural gas for reliability but leads the nation
in solar generation; its hydro output swings sharply between drought
and wet years.

## On the data

Figures are **net generation, all sectors**, from the U.S. Energy
Information Administration's electricity data (annual). "All sectors"
includes industrial and commercial self-generation, not just electric
utilities. A handful of state–fuel combinations are omitted where a
state simply doesn't generate from that source (no nuclear, no
appreciable hydro).

To see any other state's mix, open the composer and search the **state
chip** for that state's generation series.
