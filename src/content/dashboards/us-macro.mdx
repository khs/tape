## How to read this dashboard

The eight sections are ordered the way a US macro analyst typically scans a
morning briefing: **labor first** (the timeliest hard-data read on the
economy), **rates second** (because everything else is priced off them),
then **output, inflation, consumers, housing, monetary & credit, and risk
assets.**

A few cross-section reading patterns:

- **Labor + inflation + rates together.** When unemployment is low and core
  CPI/PCE is running hot, the Fed has every reason to keep policy tight.
  When unemployment rises and inflation cools, the cutting cycle starts.
  The 10Y–2Y spread is the market's forward-looking read on that arc:
  inversion historically leads recessions by 6–18 months.

- **Consumers + housing together.** Both are interest-rate sensitive.
  Retail sales weakening + housing starts falling + mortgage rates high is
  the textbook pre-recession setup. Add credit spreads widening and the
  signal is hard to ignore.

- **VIX as the meta-gauge.** A calm market (VIX in the teens) means
  participants aren't paying up for protection; a spike above 30 means
  hedging demand is overwhelming the supply. It's a near-perfect mirror
  of equity-market drawdowns but tells you something about the *price* of
  risk that levels alone don't.

## Why these eight and not others

The omitted-but-considered: PMI manufacturing + services, JOLTS detail
beyond the headline, government finance (now on its own enterprise-tier
build-out), state-level decomposition (visible in the composer — all 50
states for unemployment + payrolls), commodity prices, FX. They're all in
the composer library if you want to compose your own variant.
