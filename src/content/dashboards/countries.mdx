## How to read each chart

Every country card shows four series, all rebased to 100 at window start:

| Line | What it tells you |
|------|-------------------|
| **Country ETF** (e.g., EWJ for Japan) | How a USD investor in that country's stock market has done |
| **World ETF** (VT) | Same investor in the global benchmark |
| **Country GDP** | The economy's nominal scale in domestic currency, from World Bank |
| **World GDP** | Aggregate global output |

When a country's ETF outpaces VT, USD investors made money allocating to
it. When its GDP outpaces world GDP, the country grew faster than the
global average. **The two often disagree** — that's the interesting case.

## Patterns that recur

- **Japan and Korea**: ETF performance often understates the underlying
  economic story because of currency headwinds. Yen weakness drags EWJ
  even when Japanese GDP grows steadily.

- **China**: The decade-long divergence between Chinese GDP growth and
  Chinese-equity-ETF returns (FXI / MCHI) is one of the most striking
  pieces of macro mispricing — or correct re-pricing of governance risk —
  in recent memory.

- **India**: Both lines climbing at similar rates; one of the cleanest
  "real economic growth shows up in the equity market" cases of the
  decade.

- **Germany**: Equity flat-ish, GDP growing slowly; the chronic productivity
  problem of a manufacturing-heavy economy facing energy-cost shocks and
  China competition.

- **Mexico and Brazil**: Volatile equity ETFs reflecting currency moves
  more than fundamentals. Rebasing is essential — raw price comparisons
  would be misleading.

## What's deliberately not here

Single-country deep-dives. The composer has individual sources for each
country's GDP, ETF, currency, and stock-market indices if you want to
build a Japan-specific or India-specific dashboard. The G7 cross-country
charts (unemployment, CPI, government debt, life expectancy) are also
in the library — useful as macro context to pair with the country views
here.
