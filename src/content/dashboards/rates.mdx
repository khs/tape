## What the bond market is pricing: in five reads

The Treasury curve carries five distinct pieces of information,
and most public-facing rate commentary conflates them:

1. **Expected short-rate path.** The 10-year is approximately the
   average of expected fed-funds rates over the next ten years.
   Whatever the market expects the Fed to do, that goes here.
2. **Term premium.** Compensation for taking duration risk. Long
   bonds bounce around more than short ones; investors want extra
   yield for the volatility. Term premium isn't directly
   observable but New York Fed publishes an estimate.
3. **Inflation expectations.** Breakevens isolate this, nominal
   minus TIPS, by maturity. The 10-year breakeven is the bond
   market's expected average inflation rate over the next decade.
4. **Real expected rate.** TIPS yield is approximately the real
   expected return. When TIPS were negative (2020-21), bondholders
   were *paying* for inflation insurance.
5. **Risk premium.** Corporate-bond yields embed this on top of
   the Treasury baseline. The Treasury curve is "riskless" by
   convention; everything else is priced relative to it.

## Reading inversions

The 10y-2y curve inverts when 2-year yields exceed 10-year yields,
a market signal that the Fed is expected to tighten enough to
cause a slowdown that will require subsequent cuts. Historically:

| Inversion start | Recession start | Lead time |
|---|---|---|
| Aug 1978 | Jan 1980 | 17 months |
| Sep 1980 | Jul 1981 | 10 months |
| Jan 1989 | Jul 1990 | 18 months |
| Jul 2000 | Mar 2001 | 8 months |
| Feb 2006 | Dec 2007 | 22 months |
| Jul 2022 | ??? | TBD |

The 2022 inversion was the deepest and longest of the modern era,
and as of mid-2026 it's gone uninverted without a recession yet,
suggesting either a delayed but coming downturn, or the first
clean "soft landing" since the 1995 episode.

The 10y-3m version (Estrella-Mishkin) has a slightly stronger
historical track record because the 3-month is fully anchored to
current policy.

## Customizing for rates analysis

Common composer moves:

- **Real fed funds**: subtract us_cpi from fed_funds to get the
  inflation-adjusted policy rate.
- **Mortgage spread**: subtract ten_year from mortgage_30y to see
  how much the spread component is contributing.
- **Curve scatter**: plot 2y vs 10y as a bivariate to see how
  the curve's shape has evolved.
