## The money and the results, side by side

This dashboard pairs two questions that are usually asked separately:
how much does a state spend per K-12 student, and how well do its
students actually do?

**Spending** comes from the NCES Common Core of Data (the F-33
school-finance survey), as current spending per pupil. The spread is
striking and stable: the highest-spending states (New York,
Massachusetts) spend roughly three times per pupil what the lowest
(Utah, Idaho) do.

**Outcomes** come from NAEP, "the Nation's Report Card", the one test
administered identically in every state, which makes its scores
comparable in a way state proficiency tests are not.

## Why show them together

Line the two up and the relationship between dollars and results is
looser than the spending gaps alone would suggest:

- **Massachusetts** spends near the top *and* scores near the top, the
  case that resources and results go together.
- **Mississippi** spends well below average yet posted some of the
  country's largest NAEP gains through the 2010s (often credited to its
  early-literacy retention policy), the case that they don't.
- **Utah** spends the least per pupil and lands mid-pack on NAEP.

Spending clearly isn't *irrelevant*, but cost of living, demographics,
and policy choices drive a lot of both numbers, so per-pupil dollars
alone predict NAEP scores poorly. Treat the pairing as a starting point
for questions, not a causal claim.

## On the data

Spending is NCES Common Core of Data via the **Urban Institute
Education Data Portal** (ODC-BY 1.0; we cite both). NAEP scores are from
**NCES** directly (public domain). To pull any other state's spending or
scores, open the composer and search the **state chip**.
