## What this shows

The headline state unemployment rate hides large, persistent
differences between racial and ethnic groups. These charts pull the
rate apart for three of the largest, most diverse states, California,
Texas, and New York, plotting every group the Census reports for that
state on a single axis.

The pattern recurs almost everywhere: the Black unemployment rate runs
well above the White rate, with Hispanic workers in between and Asian
workers often at or below the White rate. Tight labor markets compress
the gaps; recessions widen them. The relative ordering rarely changes.

## On the data

The numbers come from the **Census American Community Survey (ACS)
1-year estimates**, Subject Table S2301. The ACS is a large rolling
annual household survey, it is *not* the monthly survey behind the
headline BLS unemployment rate, so these figures won't match the BLS
state numbers exactly. The trade-off buys the race and Hispanic-origin
detail at the state level, which the monthly series doesn't publish.

Smaller groups in smaller states are suppressed when the survey sample
is too thin to be reliable; that's why a given state may report fewer
lines. The three states here are large enough to report every group.

To build the same breakdown for another state, open the composer and
search the **state chip** for that state's unemployment-by-race series.
