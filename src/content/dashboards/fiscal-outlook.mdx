## What's on this dashboard

The Forecast UI ships forward-looking projections from the institutions
that publish them — not Tape-generated forecasts. In every chart, the
solid line is historical actuals and the dashed line is the projection.
The renderer extends the x-axis automatically to fit the projection's
last point.

The dashboard now spans three projection types:

- **CBO 10-year baseline** — federal outlays, revenues, deficit,
  and debt held by the public, all as a percent of GDP. Refreshes
  with each new Budget & Economic Outlook release.
- **SSA Trustees 75-year OASDI projection** — Social Security cost
  as a percent of GDP and the worker-to-beneficiary ratio.
  Intermediate-cost (Alternative II) scenario.
- **Energy commodity futures curves** — WTI crude, Brent crude, and
  Henry Hub natural gas. Each refreshes on every pipeline run with
  the latest NYMEX-quoted contract prices.

## How to read a futures curve

A futures curve isn't a forecast in the same sense as a CBO baseline
or an SSA projection. It's the actual price at which market
participants are willing to buy and sell contracts that deliver the
commodity in each future month. So it represents:

- **A consensus of market participants' expected prices**, weighted
  by who is willing to put money behind each view.
- **A risk-adjusted view** — buyers value certainty, sellers value
  storage costs and convenience yield, so the curve incorporates
  more than pure expectation.
- **An option to act**: anyone who disagrees with the curve can
  trade against it, which is why persistent curve mispricings tend
  to be eaten.

For oil specifically, the curve is the cleanest public price-forecast
available. It's not always right (1- to 4-month errors average $8-10
per barrel in WTI backtests), but it's the best price-discovery
mechanism we have.

## Customizing this dashboard

Every source on this dashboard is in the composer library. The
"Customize" link at the top opens this dashboard pre-loaded in the
composer so you can swap in alternative commodity series, add your
