## What's on this dashboard

Forward-looking views of commodity and volatility markets, all sourced
from the futures curves themselves. In every chart:

- **Solid line:** historical spot front-month, monthly averaged.
- **Dashed line:** today's forward curve, each future-month contract
  plotted at its delivery month, priced at its most recent close.

The dashed segment is not a forecast in the CBO / SSA "we built a
model and ran it" sense. It's the actual price at which traders are
willing to buy and sell contracts that physically deliver the
commodity in each future month, i.e., the market's consensus
forecast, weighted by who is willing to put money behind each view.

## How to read each curve

### Energy

Energy curves typically run smooth, WTI and Brent contracts trade
every month, and the curve's shape is dominated by inventory
expectations and stored-oil arbitrage. A persistent upward slope
("contango") implies expected appreciation, but storage costs eat
into the gain. A downward slope ("backwardation") implies tight
near-term supply.

Henry Hub natural gas has a much stronger seasonal sawtooth,
winter contracts in any given year price ~50-100% higher than the
surrounding summer months because of heating-demand peaks.

### Agriculture

Ag curves are MORE jagged than energy because the contracts deliver
in specific calendar months tied to growing cycles:

| Commodity | Delivery months |
|---|---|
| Corn | Mar, May, Jul, Sep, Dec |
| Soybeans | Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Sep, Nov |
| Wheat (CBOT) | Mar, May, Jul, Sep, Dec |
| Cotton | Mar, May, Jul, Oct, Dec |
| Live cattle | Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug, Oct, Dec |

A "discontinuity" between, say, September corn and December corn is
not a curve oddity, it's the market pricing old-crop (pre-harvest)
versus new-crop (post-harvest) supply at different levels, which is
the way ag futures are supposed to work.

### Volatility

The VIX is the market's pricing of expected S&P 500 volatility over
the next 30 days. The futures market lets traders take that view
out to ~9 months, but Yahoo only carries the front-month and
second-month continuous indices, the 2-point spread between them
is the curve's headline anyway:

- **Front < second-month** ("contango"): calm market, normal state.
- **Front > second-month** ("backwardation"): traders pay a premium
  for immediate volatility protection. Almost always a crisis signal.

Historically backwardation has appeared briefly during ~2-3 sharp
selloffs per year, and persistently during ~2 multi-month crisis
episodes per decade (2008 GFC, March 2020 COVID, August 2024 yen
carry-unwind, etc.).

## Disclaimers

Futures curves can shift sharply on macro news, geopolitical events,
and policy announcements. The dashed line is a snapshot of what
contract prices closed at on the most recent data-pipeline refresh;
it isn't a guarantee that those prices will hold. Backtests of
oil-futures forecasts versus realized prices show typical 1-to-4
month errors of $5-10 per barrel, the curve is the cleanest public
price-forecast available, but it's not precise.
