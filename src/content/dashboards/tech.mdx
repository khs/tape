## What this dashboard is for

Most "tech industry" coverage flips between two views that aren't actually
the same thing:

1. **The headline indices** (QQQ, XLK, SOXX) — what people mean when they
   say "tech is up." Mega-cap-weighted, so they really mean a handful of
   companies.
2. **The individual stocks** — the names that drive those indices. AAPL,
   MSFT, GOOG, AMZN, META, NVDA collectively account for ~25% of S&P 500
   market cap as of 2025.

This dashboard puts both side-by-side so you can see when the megacaps are
pulling the indices and when the indices are masking divergence underneath.

The credit chart is the contrarian indicator. When the equity market is
bullish on a sector and credit spreads on its junk bonds are *widening*,
something's off. Tech HY spreads widening ahead of the dot-com bust and
ahead of the 2022 Nasdaq drawdown are both visible in the long-window data.

## How concentration changed the story

Through the 2010s, "tech" was a loose label — the S&P tech sector index
(XLK) tracked similarly to the broader Nasdaq. Post-2017, the GICS sector
reclassification pulled Amazon and Meta out of "tech" into Consumer
Discretionary and Communication Services respectively, so XLK and Nasdaq
diverged. By 2024 the gap between QQQ (Nasdaq-100, includes everything)
and XLK (pure tech-classified) had grown enough that they tell visibly
different stories during AI-rally periods.

The single-name charts are most useful for tracking specific narrative
shifts: NVDA dominates the AI buildout story, AAPL has been the slowest
of the six since the iPhone supercycle plateaued, META's pivot from
metaverse to AI shows up clearly in 2022–24, GOOG's antitrust overhang
is visible as periodic drawdowns.
